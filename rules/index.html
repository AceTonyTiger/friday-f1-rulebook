Article 01

League Conduct

The standards expected of every championship participant.

1.1 Sporting behaviour

Drivers must compete fairly, honestly and in a manner that protects the reputation of the championship. Harassment, discriminatory conduct and attempts to manipulate results are prohibited.

1.2 Deliberate contact and retaliation

Intentional collisions, retaliation and deliberately forcing another car off track are prohibited and receive a race ban (0 PP). Repeated or exceptionally serious deliberate contact may result in championship disqualification (0 PP), subject to administration review.

1.3 Communications

League channels must remain constructive. Disagreement is permitted; personal attacks, public steward-bashing and abusive voice or text communications are not.

1.4 Responsibility

Entry in an official session confirms that the driver has read and accepted the current published regulations.

Article 02

Driver Registration & Attendance

Eligibility, sign-up and absence requirements.

2.1 Registration

Each driver must register with one consistent platform identity and racing number. Account sharing or racing under another entrant’s identity is prohibited.

2.2 Attendance

Drivers must confirm attendance by the published deadline. A confirmed driver who cannot race must notify league administration as soon as reasonably possible.

2.3 Reserves

Reserve seats are assigned by administration. A reserve assumes the sporting obligations of the seat for that event but does not inherit another driver’s disciplinary record.

Article 03

Race Weekend Format

The official sequence from lobby opening to classification.

3.1 Event sequence

Unless a bulletin states otherwise, an event consists of lobby checks, qualifying and the race. Administration will publish session length, weather policy and start time before the event.

3.2 Joining

Drivers must be present and ready before the lobby deadline. Joining or spectating in a way that disrupts a live qualifying lap is prohibited.

3.3 Disconnections, restarts and red flags

Individual disconnections do not normally justify a restart. Race Control or league administration may suspend, restart, reschedule or classify a race following significant technical failures or mass disconnections. No automatic restart thresholds apply. Drivers must not independently abandon a race assuming a restart will occur.

Article 04

Racing Standards

Predictable, controlled and respectful racing at all times.

4.1 Car control

A driver must remain in control and leave a reasonable margin for foreseeable changes in speed or direction. Late lunges or loss of control do not establish entitlement to racing room. Stewards assess responsibility, avoidability and consequences; a spin or retirement alone does not determine severity.

4.2 Sufficient overlap and racing room

A car is sufficiently alongside when its front axle is at least level with the other car’s rear axle at the relevant point before the corner. The same requirement applies to inside and outside overtakes. Where sufficient overlap exists, both drivers must leave at least one car’s width of racing room, provided both remain in control and can reasonably make the corner within track limits.

4.3 Defensive driving

One defensive move is permitted. Dangerous movement under braking, weaving and blocking are prohibited. Any resulting collision is assessed under the standard collision penalty system; there are no separate defensive driving penalties.

4.4 Collision assessment

Racing incidents receive No Further Action. Minor collisions receive 5 seconds (0 PP); moderate collisions receive 10 seconds (1 PP); major collisions receive 10 seconds and a qualifying ban (0 PP). Apply the definitions and consolidated schedule in Article 10.

4.5 Unsafe rejoining

A driver leaving the track or recovering from a spin must rejoin the racing line only when safe. Drivers already on circuit have priority. A minor unsafe rejoin receives 5 seconds (0 PP); a major unsafe rejoin receives 10 seconds (1 PP).

Article 05

Track Limits

Use of the circuit and the treatment of gained advantages.

5.1 Circuit boundary

The white lines define the track unless event notes specify otherwise. Drivers must make every reasonable effort to remain within them. Game-issued track-limit penalties remain applicable, subject to the existing appeal rules.

5.2 Advantage gained

A driver gaining an advantage outside track limits must return it promptly and safely. An accidental infringement without advantage receives the game penalty only (0 PP). An advantage returned safely receives No Further Action (0 PP). Retaining an advantage receives 5 seconds (0 PP); repeated or deliberate advantage receives 10 seconds (1 PP).

Article 06

Blue Flags & Qualifying Impeding

Safe passing, qualifying awareness and responsibility on track.

6.1 Blue flags

Lapped drivers must allow faster cars to pass safely at the earliest reasonable opportunity. Deliberate blocking and unpredictable movements are prohibited. The driver attempting to lap another car remains responsible for completing the overtake safely. A minor offence receives a warning (1 PP); repeated ignoring or deliberate blocking receives 5 seconds (0 PP); dangerous blocking causing collision or significant disruption receives 10 seconds (1 PP). No separate unlapping rule applies.

6.2 Qualifying impeding

Drivers on out-laps, in-laps or abandoned laps must safely give way to drivers on flying laps and avoid unnecessary obstruction. One driver on a flying lap does not automatically have priority over another also completing a flying lap. Minor accidental impeding receives a warning (1 PP); major avoidable impeding receives a qualifying ban (0 PP); severe or deliberate impeding receives a race ban (0 PP). FFC1 does not use grid-place penalties.

Article 07

Pit Lane & Retirement

Safe pit operation and retiring responsibly.

7.1 Entry and exit

Pit entry and exit must be safe and predictable. Drivers must respect applicable pit markings and avoid forcing others to take evasive action. A minor unsafe pit entry or exit receives 5 seconds (0 PP); a major offence receives 10 seconds (1 PP). Game-issued pit infringements remain subject to existing appeal rules.

7.2 Retiring on track

Drivers must return to the pits before deliberately retiring unless unable to do so safely. A first unjustified retirement on track receives a warning (1 PP); a subsequent unjustified retirement receives a qualifying ban (0 PP); deliberately retiring to disrupt another driver or session receives a race ban (0 PP). Genuine crashes, terminal damage and technical issues preventing safe return are exempt.

7.3 DNF penalty escalation

Where a driver DNFs and receives a steward-issued penalty, the first penalised DNF carries a warning (1 PP), the second a qualifying ban (0 PP), and the third a race ban (0 PP). Stewards may exempt a DNF caused by another driver or circumstances beyond the driver’s control. Each qualifying DNF race counts once regardless of penalty count. The count resets after serving the third-occurrence race ban or at season start. Original offence penalties and PP still apply; do not impose duplicate bans for the same occurrence.

Article 08

Safety Car & VSC

Conduct during neutralised race conditions.

8.1 Speed and gaps

Drivers must maintain safe speeds and reasonable gaps. Unnecessary braking, weaving, erratic acceleration, brake-checking and dangerous restart behaviour are prohibited. The race leader must not deliberately accelerate and then brake sharply to disadvantage following cars. Stewards should consider game-controlled braking and technical behaviour.

8.2 Overtaking and restart

Overtaking under safety car or virtual safety car conditions is prohibited except where directed by the game. Drivers may not create overlap or complete an overtake before the prescribed restart point.

8.3 Penalties

Minor SC/VSC misconduct receives 5 seconds (0 PP); major misconduct receives 10 seconds (1 PP); deliberate dangerous SC driving receives a race ban (0 PP).

Article 09

Reporting & Stewarding

Evidence, consistent decisions and the standards used to assess incidents.

9.1 Incident reporting

Drivers reporting an incident must provide video evidence and submit through the official channel within the published deadline, identifying the lap, drivers and alleged breach. Evidence should show sufficient time before and after the incident and, where possible, include multiple viewpoints.

9.2 Defence POV

Drivers requested by stewards to provide their defence point of view must supply it where available. Failure without a valid reason receives 1 PP. Stewards may exempt a driver with a valid reason for not providing footage. Parties must not contact stewards privately to influence a decision.

9.3 Stewarding standards

Racing Incident: Neither driver is predominantly at fault, or there is no clear breach of driving standards. Predominantly at Fault: A driver’s actions are the main cause of an avoidable incident. Shared Responsibility: Both drivers contribute significantly to an incident. Insufficient Evidence: No Further Action where responsibility cannot be established.

Outcomes alone do not determine penalty severity. Similar incidents should receive consistent penalties. The same offence must not be penalised twice. Stewards directly involved in an incident must not decide it. Every public steward decision must explain why the incident was reported, the findings and conclusion, and the penalty applied or reason for No Further Action.

10.3 Appeals

Drivers may appeal steward decisions with reasons and supporting evidence where available. Appeals are reviewed by the designated Appeals Team, with advice and input from the Head Steward. The Head Steward does not vote; the Appeals Team makes the final decision and may uphold, reduce or overturn a penalty. Points associated with an overturned decision are removed. Good-faith appeals must not be penalised merely for being submitted.

11.3 DNF classification and points

Drivers who DNF may still score championship points if they complete at least 90% of the winner’s completed race distance. Eligible drivers retain the points awarded for their classified finishing position, although their race result continues to show DNF.

11.4 Reserve drivers

Reserve drivers are eligible to score individual championship points. Points earned by reserve drivers also count towards the Constructors’ Championship.

11.5 General championship scoring

  • Driver and Constructor points follow the tables above.
  • No Driver of the Day or Moment of the Day bonus points are currently awarded.
  • All race results and championship standings use the relevant Grand Prix or Sprint points system.
  • Steward-issued time penalties may change finishing positions and therefore affect championship points.
Article 12

Lobby Settings

The controlled configuration for official sessions.

12.1 Published configuration

Assists, damage, weather, parc fermé, safety car and session settings are defined in the event bulletin or championship settings schedule.

12.2 Verification

Drivers must raise an incorrect lobby setting before the session begins. Continuing to compete may be treated as acceptance where the setting does not materially compromise fairness.

12.3 Connection quality

A persistently unstable connection that endangers others may result in removal from a session until reasonable stability is demonstrated.

Article 13

Respect for Officials

Constructive engagement with administrators and stewards.

13.1 Decisions

Drivers may request clarification or use the appeal process respectfully. Abuse, intimidation or coordinated pressure directed at an official is prohibited.

13.2 Confidentiality

Confidential steward discussions, private reports and personal information must not be published without authorisation.

13.3 Cooperation

Participants must provide truthful information when requested and comply with reasonable instructions needed to run an official event.

Article 14

League Administration

Authority and operational responsibilities of the league.

14.1 Sporting authority

Administration interprets these regulations, appoints officials, publishes calendars and manages entries in the interests of safe and fair competition.

14.2 Bulletins

An official bulletin may clarify or amend regulations. Material changes will state an effective date and be communicated through an official league channel.

14.3 Exceptional circumstances

Where circumstances are not contemplated by the rules, administration may issue a reasoned decision consistent with fairness and the intent of the championship.

Article 15

General Regulations

Final provisions governing the championship rulebook.

15.1 Precedence

Event-specific bulletins take precedence for that event. The latest published version of these regulations replaces all earlier versions.

15.2 Interpretation

Headings aid navigation but do not limit a rule’s meaning. References to a driver include their entrant or representative where appropriate.

15.3 Acceptance

Participation constitutes acceptance of the regulations, official decisions and the championship’s reasonable use of race footage and results for league operations.
